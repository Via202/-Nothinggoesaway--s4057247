WHERE DOES IT GO?

In The Story of Stuff, Annie Leonard writes, “For the great majority of these billions of tons of Stuff, there is no ‘away.’ Period.” We often stop thinking about an object as soon as we put it in the bin, as if it no longer exists. In reality, it remains somewhere, whether it is buried, burned, recycled, or moved to another place. Nothing Goes Away explores this idea through generative visuals and sound, revealing the traces that discarded objects continue to leave behind.

This idea connects to SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production, particularly its goal of reducing waste through prevention, reduction, recycling, and reuse. Waste is not only a problem that begins after something is thrown away. It is also shaped by the choices we make when buying, using, and replacing everyday objects. By showing that an object’s story continues beyond the bin, the project asks viewers to reconsider their own role in creating waste.

Before throwing something away, take a moment to ask where it will go next. Consider whether you can avoid buying it, repair it, reuse it, share it with someone else, or recycle it properly. Once an object leaves our hands, it does not stop existing. We must take responsibility for what happens to it afterward and make more careful choices before it becomes waste.
